# Bounded Cell Trial

**Test the service, not just the machine.**

This proposed trial asks whether better-defined demand can make useful component work worthwhile beside existing yard stock. It follows an identified requirement through System, a Store answer, bounded physical processing, inspection, and customer handoff.

The unit of observation is the complete job. [Machine Development](machine-development/README.md) establishes whether the cell can do the admitted work. The trial determines whether supplying it this way is useful to the customer and worthwhile for the business.

## Scope and starting conditions

The trial distinguishes three activities:

1. **Demand and baseline observation.** Record requests, customer decisions, existing alternatives, and the effort needed to clarify and prepare comparable work. This can begin before a physical cell exists.
2. **Engineering validation.** Establish references, stock control, tool processes, controller behavior, and inspected specimens for a declared capability.
3. **Bounded service testing.** Observe actual requests beside real stock using only capability released for the test. Follow preparation, processing, inspection, handoff, refusal, rework, and recovery.

Before observation, identify the participating business and location, qualifying requests, comparison methods, observation period, and request-selection method. Before powered service testing, also identify:

- Admitted stock classes, dimensions, conditions, and operations.
- The machine configuration and applicable operating release.
- Responsible operating, inspection, and test-approval roles.
- Predetermined component acceptance criteria and inspection methods.
- Stop conditions and disposition of interrupted or nonconforming work.
- Customer terms, including whether work is paid, subsidized, or experimental.
- Record custody, participant permissions, and what may be published.

Record undecided items as unresolved. No customer processing begins until the applicable conditions are met.

Requests outside the boundary remain evidence. Refuse, redirect, or record them; do not silently expand the machine to absorb them.

## Observe the whole job

Keep an identifiable record for each qualifying request, including its definition revision, Store inquiry and answer, disposition, and resulting work. Distinguish technical failures, customer decisions, and Store refusals.

| **Measure**     | **What to record**                                                                                              |
|-----------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Demand          | Request counts and types; supported, refused, redirected, deferred, and abandoned outcomes; known reasons.      |
| Preparation     | Clarification, measurement, drawing, re-entry, estimating, programming, revision, and customer communication.   |
| Material        | Parent stock, consumption, kerf, returned remnants, damage, scrap, and replacement material.                    |
| Operations      | Loading, setup, handling, machine occupancy, inspection, labeling, staging, and handoff.                        |
| Reliability     | Faults, interventions, interrupted work, recovery, rework, rejected parts, and downtime.                        |
| Support         | Tooling, maintenance, cleaning, calibration, troubleshooting, and required skills.                              |
| Economics       | Actual receipts and costs, operator occupancy, installed investment, space, software, and observed utilization. |
| Customer result | Inspection disposition, receipt and response, price offered, and whether the customer proceeded.                |

Separate observations from estimates and allocations. Count failed and refused attempts as well as completed jobs. Record shared operating time consistently so costs are neither omitted nor counted twice.

Compare realistic alternatives for the same required result: existing saw service, manual preparation, a local shop, a standard product, another supplier, repair, or refusal. Identify differences in scope and quality.

Additional demand is different from improved service. Record customer-reported reasons for starting or abandoning a project without treating them as proof of what would otherwise have happened. Interest and modeled prices are not completed purchases.

## Decide from the evidence

Before interpreting results, state the comparison and decision criteria: required component performance, acceptable service burden, the business’s margin or return requirement, and the evidence needed to continue. Record and explain later changes to those criteria.

The trial should establish:

- Which requests fit and how frequently they occur.
- Where reconstruction effort is removed, shifted, or newly introduced.
- Whether inspected components satisfy the identified requirements.
- Whether customers value the result enough to proceed on the stated terms.
- Whether the complete service justifies material, labor, equipment, maintenance, space, and operating burden at realistic utilization.
- Where another solution remains better.

A useful negative identifies the demand limit, cost, technical constraint, or better alternative. The trial must not expand merely to avoid that result.

Evidence may justify continuing, narrowing, changing, or stopping. Machine Development supplies the physical capability; this trial determines whether that capability earns a place beside the wood.
