# 3D Solutions Program

**Information now travels efficiently. Wood still doesn’t.**

**Can better-defined demand make a small amount of manufacturing worthwhile in an existing local yard, and turn more ideas into projects and more work into local income?**

3D Solutions LLC · Greensboro, North Carolina

## The North Carolina question

North Carolina still grows, cuts, and ships a great deal of wood. The work around it has concentrated. Furniture-factory employment fell **59.4% between 1993 and 2022** ([NC Commerce](https://www.commerce.nc.gov/news/the-lead-feed/not-your-grandfathers-manufacturing)). In 2024 the forest sector’s total output rose **3.1%** while total supported employment fell **1.9%** ([NC State Extension](https://content.ces.ncsu.edu/economic-contribution-of-the-forest-sector-in-north-carolina)). A 2020 Extension review describes fewer sawmill companies and a state producing more lumber than it consumed ([sawmill review](https://content.ces.ncsu.edu/the-north-carolina-sawmill-industry-a-closer-look)).

Scale, costs, trade, and technology shape where work remains worthwhile. For decades, much of manufacturing investment favored larger facilities and concentrated production. Precise digital definitions, accessible motion control, and dependable software may now make a different scale worthwhile: one board, one opening, one customer’s result.

**What if the information reached local wood and modest automation first?** [▶ Open the working demonstration](https://system-candidate-production.up.railway.app/) and see how the customer’s own project definition drives the cut, mill, and drill beside inventory already sitting in a yard. The work, and some of the income, could happen closer to home.

**The customer writes the definition. Nobody at the yard interprets the project or programs the order.**

In a typical CNC workflow, each job carries preparation work of its own: interpretation, programming and machine-specific instruction generation. The architecture described in the [issued patents](EVIDENCE.md#architecture-and-provenance) approaches that burden differently. The machine, its material and its permitted operations are prepared and registered in advance: tools, stations, reference positions and limits, with the yard’s stock matched to what they can do. A customer defining work against those known capabilities is therefore defining what becomes the machine program. In the intended chain, a registered local compiler generates the instructions from the accepted definition.

The question for this Program is not whether the architecture can be described. It is whether removing enough of that per-job burden makes a bounded local service economically worthwhile.

This Program’s proposition is **distributed manufacturing at the scale of an existing yard, beginning with a bounded service that could earn its keep.** The yard already has wood, handling equipment, customers, and people who know the business. The working application already supports job definition and Store evaluation. The next increment is a small dimensional-stock cell, with local machine integration and physical validation.

## Make the project easier to start

A person needs a fitted shelf, a replacement brace, a panel with an opening, or pieces for a small assembly. They can picture the result. Getting suitable material and accurately prepared parts is the hard part. For a modest project, arranging the work can cost more effort than the work itself.

The opportunity begins upstream of the machine: help people define what they want and ask a local business what it can supply. The customer takes home pieces they can build with. The yard sells both the wood and the work that makes it useful.

Some projects already happen and could be served better. Others stay ideas because getting the right pieces feels too hard. Both have to pay off in the same job: useful to the customer, worthwhile for the yard.

A defined request can already move through the working application to Store evaluation, material selection, a priced quote, modeled machine time and generated virtual commands. The remaining question is physical execution: can the same definition move from customer request to inspected component without repeated reconstruction, separate machine programming or manual reinterpretation?

**Define it once. Nobody downstream should have to redraw it.**

The tools and control methods are familiar.

## What would make it worthwhile

The smallest useful cell is set by actual requests and the full cost of supplying them: material, preparation, handling, processing, inspection, people, maintenance, investment, and realistic utilization. Compare it with an existing tool, shop, supplier, or manual service that may serve the customer better.

The [Supervised Laboratory Dimensional Cell Trial](SUPERVISED-LABORATORY-CELL-TRIAL.md) follows real requests through the complete job. A good result is useful work supplied at a worthwhile margin. A useful negative identifies what stands in the way.

## What has been shown

Try **Start your own** in the application. Intent establishes the job’s requirements; the Bench lets you work their values. Then follow the [Project 1 digital manufacturing trail](https://github.com/GeorgePlattDemo/store-zero/blob/main/docs/project-1-digital-trail/D001_Project1_Review.md) from one definition to a reproduced Store answer and generated virtual commands. The [verification record](https://github.com/GeorgePlattDemo/new-system/blob/main/docs/verification/start-your-own.md) describes what has been shown for Start your own, and what has not.

The application is a working software demonstration. Store Zero’s prices and machine time are modeled. Project 1 supplies a digital reference specimen; physical capability and commercial viability remain to be demonstrated.
