# 3D Solutions Program

**Information travels anywhere. Wood doesn’t.**

**Can better-defined demand make a small amount of manufacturing worthwhile in an existing local yard, and turn more ideas into projects and more work into local income?**

3D Solutions LLC · Greensboro, North Carolina

[▶ Open the working demonstration](https://georgeplattdemo.github.io/scan-to-build-system/system-build-current.html)

## The North Carolina question

North Carolina still grows, cuts, and ships a great deal of wood. The work around it has concentrated. Furniture-factory employment fell **59.4% between 1993 and 2022** ([NC Commerce](https://www.commerce.nc.gov/news/the-lead-feed/not-your-grandfathers-manufacturing)). In 2024 the forest sector’s total output rose **3.1%** while total supported employment fell **1.9%** ([NC State Extension](https://content.ces.ncsu.edu/economic-contribution-of-the-forest-sector-in-north-carolina)). A 2020 Extension review describes fewer sawmill companies and a state producing more lumber than it consumed ([sawmill review](https://content.ces.ncsu.edu/the-north-carolina-sawmill-industry-a-closer-look)).

Scale, costs, trade, and technology shape where work remains worthwhile. For decades, much of manufacturing investment favored larger facilities and concentrated production. Precise digital definitions, accessible motion control, and dependable software may now make a different scale worthwhile: one board, one opening, one customer’s result.

**What if the information reached local wood and modest automation first?** The customer’s own project definition could drive the cut, mill, and drill beside inventory already sitting in a yard. The work, and some of the income, could happen there.

That is this Program’s proposition: **distributed manufacturing at the scale of an existing yard, beginning with a bounded service that could earn its keep.** The yard already has wood, handling equipment, customers, and people who know the business. The working application already supports job definition and Store evaluation. The next increment is a small dimensional-stock cell, with local machine integration and physical validation.

## Make the project easier to start

A person needs a fitted shelf, a replacement brace, a panel with an opening, or pieces for a small assembly. They can picture the result. Getting suitable material and accurately prepared parts is the hard part. For a modest project, arranging the work can cost more effort than the work itself.

The opportunity begins upstream of the machine: help people define what they want and ask a local business what it can supply. The customer takes home pieces they can build with. The yard sells both the wood and the work that makes it useful.

Some projects already happen and could be served better. Others stay ideas because getting the right pieces feels too hard. Both have to pay off in the same job: useful to the customer, worthwhile for the yard.

## Demand as Architecture

[Demand as Architecture](research/demand-as-architecture.md) asks what changes when a need becomes usable information before it becomes an order.

1. **Define the result.** Establish the parts, features, dimensions, and operations needed.
2. **Ask the local Store.** It answers from its material, capability, work model, and economics.
3. **Carry the accepted definition into manufacturing.** Local machine engineering supplies the instructions for identified equipment.
4. **Return useful components.** Prepare, identify, inspect, and hand them over for assembly.

**Define it once. Nobody downstream should have to redraw it.**

The economic proposition is to remove repeated reconstruction, separate machine-file preparation, and reconciliation from routine supported jobs. The tools and control methods are familiar. What needs testing is whether a job arriving already defined removes enough burden to make the service worthwhile.

## What would make it worthwhile

The smallest useful cell is set by actual requests and the full cost of supplying them: material, preparation, handling, processing, inspection, people, maintenance, investment, and realistic utilization. Compare it with an existing tool, shop, supplier, or manual service that may serve the customer better.

The [bounded-cell trial](research/bounded-cell-trial.md) follows real requests through the complete job. A good result is useful work supplied at a worthwhile margin. A useful negative identifies what stands in the way.

## Bring a real case

Bring one request a yard keeps turning away, or one project someone would start if the right pieces were available. Customers can establish the need and what they would pay. Dealers can establish service demand and operating realities. Workers and educators can examine the tasks and skills. Engineers can test the capability; researchers can compare the complete economics.

[Bring a case, question, or observation](https://github.com/GeorgePlattDemo/3d-solutions-program/discussions/1), or share these repositories with someone who can test part of the proposition.
