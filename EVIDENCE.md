# Evidence

*Defined Demand, Local Wood, Useful Work · 3D Solutions Program · Proposed evidence-first review · 19 October 2026*

## North Carolina’s changing wood economy

*Historical evidence — reported by public agencies and identified industry sources*

NC Commerce’s **Not Your Grandfather’s Manufacturing**, published August 31, 2023, reports that North Carolina furniture-factory employment fell **59.4% between 1993 and 2022**. It describes globalization, trade, efficiency and automation in the wider manufacturing transition. The employment decline is documented; no single cause or means of recovering those jobs follows from that percentage. [1]

NC State Extension’s **Economic Contribution of the Forest Sector in North Carolina, 2024** reports total forest-sector output **up 3.1%** and total supported employment **down 1.9%** from 2023. It reports approximately **66,508 direct jobs** and **140,919 jobs including indirect and induced effects**. These are sector-wide measures, not the furniture-factory employment series, and the opposing annual movements do not by themselves identify a cause. [2]

Extension’s **The North Carolina Sawmill Industry: A Closer Look** documents a decline from **229 sawmill companies in 2001 to the low 130s by the late 2010s**. It also describes a state producing more lumber than it consumes, industry consolidation and changing output and employment. The number of firms is not a measure of production volume or an interchangeable employment count. [3]

A North Carolina Forest Service historical assessment separately reports forest-industry-related employment near **120,000 in 1990** and **77,000 in 2008**. Its population and definitions must remain attached to that historical series; those numbers cannot be spliced into the 2024 Extension estimates as if they were one continuous measure. [4]

Individual facilities make the scale issue concrete. Weyerhaeuser indefinitely curtailed its New Bern sawmill in Vanceboro in 2024, affecting **73 employees**, and cited market conditions, cost structure, lack of scale and limited integration. [5] A **March 2026 WARN notice** for Prepac’s Whitsett plant identified **200 planned permanent layoffs**. A WARN notice is evidence of a notified action, not proof of how many workers subsequently separated. [6]

These reports establish a changing regional wood economy and consequential losses of manufacturing work. They do **not** establish that local wood preparation would reverse those losses, create net new jobs, or attract paying customers. Those are separate propositions requiring evidence.

## What independent manufacturing research establishes

*Published research — different products and boundaries, relevant economic mechanisms*

Industrial concentration has an economic basis. Zhang and Lan’s study of cross-laminated timber (CLT) production in the Southern United States modeled an increase in annual plant capacity from **30,000 to 150,000 m³**. Modeled capital increased **203%**, while labor cost per cubic meter fell **$148.0 to $37.8** and median minimum selling price fell **$571 to $376/m³**. Transportation accounted for **1%–8%** of modeled minimum selling price, and the largest plant was not invariably the lowest-cost *delivered* option. Those are CLT plant findings, **not** cost estimates for a lumberyard, two braces or the proposed dimensional machine. [7]

Srai and colleagues’ 2016 **Distributed manufacturing: scope, challenges and opportunities** examines how digitally connected production, local resources, infrastructure, customization, inventory and logistics can alter where manufacturing takes place. Its analysis does not say that smaller or nearer production always costs less. Local customization may carry a higher unit manufacturing cost even where it improves flexibility or reduces other burdens. [8]

Helu and colleagues’ 2020 NIST-linked **Industry Review of Distributed Production in Discrete Manufacturing** identifies a specific obstacle to varied, low-volume production: quotations require human planning; information about requirements and capability can be lost across systems; engineering and process development recur. It also emphasizes correct, verifiable production where a small order cannot absorb repeated scrap. These findings identify preparation and coordination as real cost categories, without measuring their reduction in this Program. [9]

A 2025 IFAC wood-prefabrication study by Zwingmann and colleagues presents a related engineering mechanism: structured parametric definitions are turned into manufacturing features, operations and machine-specific instructions using known production capabilities, with a machining preview. Its demonstrated setting is wood-building prefabrication. It is independent support for a *type of information flow*, not an independently measured Scan-to-Build result. [10]

Together, these studies establish two conditions that must be held in tension: **scale reduces some production costs**, while **preparation, coordination and delivery can change the cost of varied orders**. None identifies the break-even utilization, material mix or customer demand for a small registered wood-processing service.

NC State Industry Extension Services and the North Carolina Manufacturing Extension Partnership document regional resources for industrial assessment and technology adoption. Their existence is relevant to possible independent evaluation; **neither is represented as a participant in, or endorser of, this Program**. [28]

## What the enabling-technology price record shows

*Dated market comparators — not installed-machine prices or machine-accuracy evidence*

AutomationDirect’s September 2005 example priced a **400 W low-inertia servo drive, unbraked motor and 10-ft cable package at $996**. A specified February 11, 2026 SureServo2 comparator comprises a **$421 drive**, **$374 motor**, **$72 power cable** and **$95 feedback cable**: **$962** in total. [11, 12]

Using the stated CPI-U figures (**198.8** for September 2005 and **325.252** for January 2026), the older $996 is **$1,629.53** in January 2026 general purchasing power. The 2026 package is thus **40.96% lower in CPI-adjusted price** despite a nominal decline of only about **3.4%**. The 2005 release does not identify matching part numbers, the generations differ, the cable lengths are only approximately equivalent, and CPI-U is **not an equipment-specific deflator**. Installation, controller integration, guarding, tooling and tax are not priced here. These qualifications are necessary to the comparison. [11–14]

The documented internal feedback counts differ from **10,000** to **16,777,216 counts/revolution**, approximately **1,678-fold**. Separate position measurement is also commercially accessible: the cited examples include encoder-wheel systems starting at **$335**, linear readheads starting at **$207** with tape and integration additional, and a selected SICK wheel specified at **200 mm per 3,600 steps**, or a nominal **0.0556 mm/step**. Those numbers describe feedback resolution and catalog availability, **not calibrated movement of lumber, restraint, datum retention or achieved cut accuracy**. [12, 14–16]

Another cost category has changed. Stanford’s 2025 AI Index reports a GPT-3.5-level MMLU inference-price threshold falling from **$20 to $0.07 per million tokens** between November 2022 and October 2024—approximately **286-fold**. A separate 2026 ProCAD preprint reports simulated-user CAD invalidity of **4.8% versus 0.9%** in a full-system comparison. Their populations, tasks and measures differ. Neither establishes that AI can safely resolve an arbitrary wood plan or generate valid machine motion. The current application does **not** claim unrestricted AI plan ingestion; bounded controls and deterministic rules remain its basis. [17, 18, R2, R4]

This record supports a change in some **enabling-cost comparisons**, not a finding that the proposed machine has been purchased, integrated, calibrated or made safe at a particular cost. The selected controller bill of materials, maintenance burden and inspected precision remain to be measured. [R1]

## What material journeys and customer-value research show

*Published environmental and behavioral findings — distinct denominators, products and study boundaries*

Freight is part of a complete delivered result. It is not enough to compare the cutting cost at two facilities while ignoring how material reaches them and how the finished work reaches the customer. Conversely, local production can add customer trips, small-load deliveries and less efficient procurement. An environmental percentage is not a cash saving, and a shorter leg is not necessarily a smaller overall footprint.

A U.S. Forest Products Laboratory southeastern-hardwood example attributes **14% of 209 kg CO₂e/m³** to raw-material transportation within its cradle-to-mill-gate boundary. That boundary excludes downstream delivery. [19] Yang and colleagues’ 2025 review of **25 furniture cases** reports distribution accounting for an average **9% of combined environmental impact scores**, with an approximate **0.01%–27%** range. This is a *multi-impact score*, **not** 9% of greenhouse-gas emissions. [20]

Wang and colleagues’ modeled **80-kg bed** identifies material-freight emissions of **6.95 + 114.53 + 40.76 kg CO₂e** and finished-product-freight emissions of **4.07 + 0.16 kg CO₂e**: **166.47 kg CO₂e**, or **35.9% of its 464.04 kg CO₂e** total. Packaging production separately accounts for **14.98 kg CO₂e**. The case concerns a different supply chain and product and does **not** by itself establish any North Carolina alternative or ocean return to the United States. [21]

An independently verified Allwood flooring environmental product declaration documents **American walnut and hickory** shipped into production of engineered flooring in China for U.S. customers. Its modeled finished-product travel includes **9,500 km by ship and 1,600 km by truck**, or **11,100 km**, in addition to upstream raw-material journeys. This documents a real export-and-processing-return *pattern*. It does not establish North Carolina origin, how common that journey is, or the freight that a local brace-making service would avoid. [22]

Customer value has a separate evidence base. Franke, Schreier and Kaiser report stated bids of **€6.85 versus €4.75** for self-designed shirts—approximately **44.2% higher**, with reported groups **n=33/38** and **p=0.01**. [24] Norton, Mochon and Ariely report **$0.78 versus $0.48**, approximately **62.5% higher**, for a self-assembled box (**n=52; p<0.05**). [25] These controlled comparisons concern participation and valuation, **not the selling price customers will pay for these wood components**.

Mugge and colleagues’ personalization study includes **149 bicycle participants**. A separate attachment study reports **115 watch-scenario responses**, with **r=0.44, p<0.01** between product attachment and an **expected** retention category. Neither records observed extra years of service for woodwork or proves a lower disposal rate. [26, 27]

The published record therefore identifies **possible differences in logistics, environmental burden, willingness to pay and attachment**. These effects cannot be added as percentages. They are measured on different products, populations and boundaries. To establish their relevance here requires comparable real orders, actual freight charges, observed customer decisions, repair and replacement histories, and a consistent definition of useful service.

## Source words, manufacturing meaning and the refusal boundary

*Adopted engineering requirements; bounded software evidence; physical qualification outstanding*

Manufacturing plans can call a face miter a “bevel,” a pointed shallow depression a “spotface,” or a “pilot hole” without specifying its size or depth. The original language, source drawing, page and revision remain evidence, but a word alone cannot create physical geometry or registered machine capability. Missing end-plane relationships, datums, locations, depths and contradictions must be exposed—not guessed, renamed to force success or silently discarded. General unstructured PDF/CAD plan ingestion is **not** an established current function. [R2, R4]

System owns the customer's identified requirements, geometry, features, manifest, derivations, interpretation and version. Store owns declared stock, eligibility, registered manufacturing capability, planning, modeled time/Q, receipt and attributable refusal. The required chain is **published definition → independent manifest → System translation → shared Store transport → fresh evaluation by the pinned Store → identified response and customer decision → accepted packet → verified registered lowering and virtual evidence**. Physical release and independent inspection are later, distinct decisions. [R2–R5]

A local duplicate price engine, a manufactured stock answer, a whole-answer cache, a dropped feature or an altered packet cannot serve as Store evidence. An unsupported request must be refused with a reason; a disagreement between implementation and adopted contract must be recorded as a defect rather than papered over. The five public demonstrations are bounded examples, not five exemptions to the same manufacturing contract. [R2–R5]

One-roller contact states, registered retained-face and kerf relationships, and saw downstroke geometry remain outstanding physical obligations. **Physical admission is BLOCKED.** Controller export, machine restraint, verified travel, guards, inspection, release governance and operator authority remain separately demonstrable. Software verification is not a substitute for any of them. [R1–R4]

## What the complete economic comparison must count

*Declared model and sensitivity calculations — not observed profitability*

The proper economic unit is **one equivalent, usable customer result**, not a single cut, spindle minute or parent-board price. Hold required geometry, tolerance, useful performance, quantity and delivery terms constant when comparing yard preparation, manual work, a local shop, conventional suppliers, a standard product, repair, or a documented refusal. Record recurring clarification and planning, material and remnants, loading, processing, inspection, rework, staging, packaging, customer travel, freight, software support, maintenance, capital, premises, downtime and realistic utilization. Avoid counting one activity twice. [R1, R3]

Store's declared **$120,000 annual cost pool** comprises **$50,000 operator**, **$25,000 capital**, **$20,000 premises**, **$15,000 maintenance/tooling** and **$10,000 energy/dust/IT**. At **600 modeled productive hours/year**, the allocated internal burden is **$200/hour**; applying a **20% gross margin on selling price** gives a **$250/hour selling rate**. These are reference assumptions, not actual utilization, purchased equipment costs, paid wages or completed transactions. Selling price and internal resource cost must stay distinct. [R3]

If order clarification and preparation fall from **15 to 2 minutes** at an assumed **$40/hour**, the difference is **13/60 × $40 = $8.67 per order**. Additional annual support of **$10,000** would consume approximately **1,154** such savings unless already included in the $120,000 pool. The modeled 16-in specimen counts **60 of 86.519 occupied seconds** as handling (**69.35%**); adding handling again as though absent from Store's modeled work would double count. These are sensitivity checks, not witnessed savings or a measured break-even point. [R3, R13]

Stock has a second accounting boundary. At actual nominal-2×4 dimensions of **1.5 × 3.5 in**, a **60-in** parent contains **2.1875 physical board feet** and a **72-in** parent contains **2.625 physical board feet**. Count full parent commitment, kerf, defects, recoverable offcuts, reuse and disposal by observed disposition. Store does not currently credit a hypothetical remnant's future sale. [R3, R11]

### Carbon miles: trace the movement before claiming an avoided journey

The four different measures below answer four different questions; none can be substituted for another. [19–23]

| Measure | Calculation or record | What it establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Physical board-foot miles (BF·mi)** | Σ(physical board feet × miles per leg) | Volume-distance accounting; **not** dollars or emissions |
| **Ton-miles** | Σ(allocated transported tons × miles per leg) | Freight mass-distance; useful input to mode-specific emissions models |
| **Freight dollars per completed result** | Paid or allocated freight, loading, packaging, backhaul and customer collection | Economic impact including minimum charges and empty trips |
| **kg CO₂e per equivalent useful result** | Mode/fuel/vehicle/load factors over a declared lifecycle boundary | Environmental impact, separate from cash cost and carbon stored in wood |

For illustration, a **2.1875-board-foot** 60-in parent traveling a hypothetical **100 miles** accumulates **218.75 BF·mi**; a **2.625-board-foot** 72-in parent on the same hypothetical leg accumulates **262.5 BF·mi**. This is a dimensional calculation, **not an observed wood shipment or an estimate of avoided emissions**. [R3]

A comparable route ledger should cover forest/source processor, sawmill/dryer/wholesaler, receiving yard, off-site or local fabrication, storage, packaging, retailer, customer collection or delivery, and any remnant/reverse movement. Preserve **origins, destinations, distance, mass, volume, transport mode, vehicle load, allocated shipment share, empty return or backhaul, packaging, losses and actual charges** for *both* the local and conventional alternatives. Select and disclose the applicable EPA SmartWay ton-mile or vehicle-mile method and version rather than manufacturing a generic CO₂ factor. [23]

For product retention, **15 versus 10 years** of equivalent useful function reduces the same assumed one-time production-and-delivery burden **per service-year by 33.3%** (**1 − 10/15**). This is arithmetic, **not evidence that customer participation extends actual product life**. Maintenance, coatings, repairs and replacements must be included before making a lifecycle comparison. [24–27]

## What has to happen before the evidence changes category

*Future work — supervised tests, not a production announcement*

The [Supervised Laboratory Dimensional Cell Trial](SUPERVISED-LABORATORY-CELL-TRIAL.md) provides a bounded method for gathering missing evidence. Its candidate dimensional-stock cell includes controlled stock travel, proposed feed/clamp rollers, fence and support references, saw, router and shallow-spot functions, locally registered controller interfaces and safeguards determined by risk assessment. Its approximately **5 × 3 ft** figure describes a *core machine target*, **not** the floor space required for infeed/outfeed, guarding, access or safe operation. [R1]

The trial's evidentiary progression is explicit:

1. **Observe demand and the baseline.** Keep real requests, unsupported work, refusals, abandoned projects, changes, alternative providers, actual preparation time and customer decisions. Interest and subsidized laboratory uptake are not paid demand.
2. **Qualify physical execution.** Establish machine identity, controller mapping, registration, one- and two-roller contact behavior, stock restraint, independent travel measurement, reference retention, end/kerf geometry, saw downstroke, shallow-spot depth, guarding and emergency response. No software quotation or accepted packet permits physical motion. Inspect actual components against predetermined tolerances.
3. **Repeat identified specimens.** Tie the current **16-in and 18-in SPF** definitions and admitted shallower-spot reference to their manifest, Store receipt, selected stock, executable configuration, measured cycle and independently inspected outcome. Keep the older treated-lumber specimen historical.
4. **Measure whole-service cost and willingness to pay.** Record total preparation, programming, handling, scrap, rework, inspection, support, idle time, depreciation, space, maintenance, freight, customer travel and delivery. Then investigate an identified willing yard with real material, people, price and utilization. Free cutting and bring-your-own-wood demand do not establish a profitable service.
5. **Compare equivalent outcomes.** Report cash cost, delivery burdens, observed paid choices and environmental effects on a symmetric boundary, including occasions when a nonlocal alternative is cheaper or cleaner. Preserve failures, abandoned orders and negative findings. [R1–R5]

A proposed **capability-impact engine** is a separate future function: retain rejected and unresolved customer work, evaluate hypothetical additional registered tools, ranges, stations or machines against that same corpus, and compare newly feasible work with investment, maintenance, labor and utilization. Tomorrow's hypothetical Store must not appear as a current offer. Adding a third or fourth machine should extend capability registration and execution without reconstructing the customer's definition compiler. No such service is represented here as operational. [R1–R5]

**Decision standard.** A favorable decision requires repeatedly conforming inspected components under authorized conditions, real evidence of the preparation/support burden, paid demand at realistic utilization and competitive *complete* delivered results. A negative decision is an acceptable research result. **NO BLOOD ON WOOD.**

## The case the evidence permits the Program to make

The historical employment record explains why this matters in North Carolina. The CLT study explains why factories grew large; distributed-manufacturing research identifies burdens that size alone does not settle. Independent pricing records show that some tools of precise control and information processing have become more accessible. Freight studies show that material movement can be economically and environmentally consequential. Customer-participation studies identify possible value outside the price of a standardized product. **None of these authors studied or endorsed this Program. Their separate findings illuminate different parts of a problem worth testing; they do not collectively prove its proposed answer.** [1–28]

The patents describe the earlier architecture. The two separately identified Project 1 generations, and the current additional component test, show that a bounded customer definition can continue into stock choice, operation geometry, modeled time, price and nominal motion without being redesigned at every handoff. The record also says exactly where the proof stops: before commissioned cutting, inspection, observed demand and commercial economics. [R2–R12]

**Here is the Program's proposition:** The manufacturing knowledge of a modest registered machine can be established and maintained in advance. A customer can then define supported work against that capability, and the System and an independent Store can carry the same identified requirements into a manufacturing plan without requiring a CAD/CAM designer to reprogram each accepted order. If this removes enough *recurring preparation*, it may lower the cost of variety enough to make a useful share of otherwise avoided woodwork economical near existing yard stock.

That is not a proposal to outproduce a furniture factory, abolish skilled work or make a customer into a machine operator. The yard can sell both material and the work that makes it useful. The customer can receive pieces suited to a real need rather than undertake an engineering project just to obtain them. The machine's limits are registered; refusal is legitimate; humans remain responsible for safe qualification, operation, inspection and release.

There may be another benefit. If more useful processing takes place near suitable stock and the customer, some avoidable journeys, packaging, handling and wasted effort may disappear. If people participate in defining and completing what they need, some may value, maintain or retain it longer. These are **testable benefits**, not already-booked freight savings, carbon credits or lifetime extensions.

**The next proof is wood, not words:** a real request, an identified machine, a conforming inspected component, the complete cost of supplying it, and a customer prepared to pay. A service that cannot earn its keep should not be declared viable; a capability that cannot operate safely should not run.

**Information travels anywhere. Wood doesn’t.** The purpose of the experiment is to determine when keeping useful work closer to the wood—and to the person who needs it—makes economic and practical sense.
